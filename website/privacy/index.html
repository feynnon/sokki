Sokki turns speech into text and writes notes on your Mac. It has no account and no server that holds your meetings.

Keep what I write is on: when you send or leave a field in another app, Sokki keeps its text, the app’s name and the time in its folder on your Mac. Never password fields, anything typed while macOS hides your keys (as in password managers), terminals or Sokki itself. It is never uploaded or shared. Turn it off or Delete all in Settings › Developer mode. With Learn my words (on by default; turn it off in Settings › Developer mode), a model on your Mac adds names and terms from it to your Vocabulary.

Start meetings by themselves is on, once Sokki knows your own voice (after you name yourself in a meeting and keep your voice). While nothing records, Sokki then listens on your Mac’s built-in microphone, and macOS shows its orange dot. It starts a meeting only when it hears your voice talking for a while: not for other voices, not during a call (Sokki asks then), a dictation or while an app plays sound. The last 35 seconds stay in memory only, never written to disk or sent. When it hears you, it first records the first minute into memory only; only a conversation that goes on becomes a meeting, and one under two minutes is deleted when it stops. Turn it off with the Auto start switch on the card that appears when a meeting starts by itself.

Every request shows the server your IP address, as any connection does. Never sent: your name, your Mac’s name or serial number, your screen video.

Model downloads

When you pick a speech or language model, Sokki downloads it once from Hugging Face, or from Handy’s mirror (blob.handy.computer) if that fails. Then it runs offline.

Updates

A minute after start and every 4 hours, Sokki asks GitHub whether there is a new version. If there is, it downloads it, checks its signature and installs it after your first login of the day, when nothing is recording. Turn it off in Settings › Debug › Check for Updates.

Messages from Sokki

A minute after start and every 6 hours, Sokki downloads one small file from its counter (a Cloudflare Worker): how many people share, and a message from Sokki, if there is one. It sends nothing with it.

Help improve Sokki

Off until you turn it on. Then, under a random ID, once a day:

  • Sokki’s version, the models and languages you chose
  • your Mac’s model, chip, cores, memory, disk size and free space, macOS version and region setting
  • how many dictations and meetings you had, how long, in which language, how many speakers, which meeting app, which day
  • warnings and errors from Sokki’s log, with quoted words and your home folder blanked
  • which setup steps you reached and when, when you first dictated, updates
  • which features you use and how often (dictation, meetings, summaries, files), and switches and fixed choices in Settings, never what you typed into them
  • crashes (where in Sokki’s code, no text), and how fast dictations and meetings are ready

Sokki counts these on your Mac and sends them only once this is on; the first report includes what was counted before, such as setup. The counter passes the report on to PostHog in Frankfurt for charts, without your IP address. Delete what was shared deletes it there too.

With this or Share all my recordings on, at most hourly: how many of your own recordings you keep, and how many hours per language. Never audio, never anyone’s words.

Share all my recordings

Off until you turn it on. Then Sokki uploads every recording of your own voice on this Mac, unchanged, with its transcript. Recordings can contain names, numbers and what people near you said. Sokki may use them to improve Sokki and for open speech models, and may share or sell them.

Ask me when there’s a buyer

Off until you turn it on. Sokki compares buyer messages with the hours you keep, on your Mac. Nothing leaves until you answer I’m interested; then Sokki sends the random ID, the message, the language and the matching hours.

Phone link

Off until you turn on Accept recordings from my phone. Your phone then sends recordings straight to this Mac, end-to-end encrypted. To find the Mac, the link registers its public key with n0’s address service; when no direct connection is possible, n0’s relays pass the encrypted bytes along without being able to read them. Nothing is stored in between. The pairing code stays after the # in the sokki.org link, which browsers never send to a server.

Cloud language model

Sokki writes notes with a model on your Mac. If you pick Anthropic, OpenAI or OpenRouter for meeting notes instead, the transcript goes to that provider. If you turn on dictation cleanup with a cloud provider (Advanced settings), the dictated text goes to it. You sign up with that provider and use your own key; its privacy terms apply.

Connected accounts

Nothing here happens until you connect an account, and you can disconnect at any time.

Gmail

To send a meeting’s note to the people who were in it, from your own address, Sokki asks Google for two things: to send email on your behalf (gmail.send), and your email address, to show which account is connected. Sokki can’t read, search or delete your mail.

  • What Sokki does with it: after each meeting, sends its note (summary, decisions and action items, never the transcript) from your Gmail to the people you named as speakers in that meeting, at the address on their Speakers page or in your calendar invite (after a Slack huddle, everyone in the huddle). Never to anyone you didn’t name, and never to addresses on your No mail to list. This is on by default; turn off Send to everyone after each meeting in Settings › Meetings. A note’s Send to everyone button sends it by hand. Nothing else.
  • Where it’s kept: Google’s access token stays in your Mac’s Keychain. The token, your address and your notes never reach Sokki’s servers or anyone other than Google and your recipients.
  • Never: shared, sold, used for ads, or used to train AI models.
  • Disconnect: in Sokki’s settings, or at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Sokki’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Apple Mail

Without Gmail, Sokki sends the same mails through the Mail app on your Mac, from your own account. If someone answers a note with “stop”, Sokki stops mailing them; for that it reads the first line of such replies, on your Mac. Nothing of this leaves your Mac.

Slack

When you connect Slack, Sokki may post messages and see huddles in your workspace, so it can post a huddle’s note into its thread. Slack’s token stays in your Mac’s Keychain; the note goes only to Slack.

Calendar

With your permission, Sokki reads your Mac’s calendar through macOS to name meetings and know who attended. Calendar data stays on your Mac. Attendees’ addresses leave only in the mails Sokki sends for you.

Delete everything

On the Your data page: delete your recordings, and Delete what was shared to remove everything the counter holds of your Mac; this also turns sharing off. Transcripts and counts are deleted at once; the audio files within a few days at most. Meeting notes are plain files: delete them like any file.

This website

No cookies, no tracking script, no fonts from other servers. When sokki.org delivers a page, it counts it, and clicks on its download and GitHub links: the page, the site that linked here, the link’s tag (like ?s=note, ref or utm_ tags), your country, your browser type, and a visitor number that changes every day. Your IP address is used only to make that number and is never stored. PostHog keeps the counts in Frankfurt and processes them for us under a data processing agreement.

It’s hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may log visitors’ IP addresses for security. See GitHub’s privacy statement.

Requests to sokki.org pass through Cloudflare, which may log IP addresses for security, hides email addresses from spam bots with a small script, and may ask your browser to report connection errors to it. See Cloudflare’s privacy policy.

Who and why

Responsible: RD Holding UG (haftungsbeschränkt), Loristraße 12, 80335 München, hello@sokki.org.

  • Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR): Help improve Sokki, Share all my recordings, I’m interested, the phone link, cloud models and connected accounts. Turn the switch off or disconnect to withdraw it.
  • Our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) in a working, safe, up-to-date app and website: model downloads, updates, messages from Sokki, and knowing how many people visit sokki.org and where they come from.

Who processes it: Cloudflare runs the counter and keeps shared data (R2 in the EU, Workers KV and D1) until you delete it; it counts writes per IP address in memory to stop abuse and stores none. GitHub hosts the website and the updates, Cloudflare passes on requests to it, PostHog keeps its visitor counts and the Help improve Sokki reports in Frankfurt. Hugging Face and Handy’s mirror host the models, n0 the phone link’s address service and relays. Cloudflare, GitHub, PostHog and Hugging Face are US companies.

Your rights: ask what is stored under your Mac’s random ID, have it corrected or deleted, restrict it, take it with you, object to it, and complain to a data protection authority, such as the Bayerisches Landesamt für Datenschutzaufsicht.
